Examples

Design For Impact in action.

These fictitious examples show how the same five components can support collaborations with different goals, stakeholders, and definitions of impact. They are illustrations, not templates: each project should adapt the tool to its own context.

Marketing Campaign Optimization

A statistical practitioner is helping a marketing team identify customer segments for an upcoming campaign and decide how to allocate its budget.

Vision — Q1

Maximize customer engagement and lifetime value through more personalized marketing strategies.

Impact — Q1

Help the marketing team allocate its budget across customer segments to maximize conversion.

Partnership — Q1

Deliver within two weeks, before the next campaign-planning cycle begins.

Analysis Pathway — Q2

Fit a logistic model to estimate conversion probability across customer segments, accompanied by interpretable visualizations that guide budgetary decisions.

Narrative — Q3

The model identified a customer segment with a significantly higher likelihood of conversion. Marketing shifted its budget toward this segment, increasing overall conversion from 8% to 14% and generating $86,000 in additional revenue.

What this illustrates: The business goal, stakeholder decision, project timeline, analysis, and final result remain connected throughout the engagement.

Environmental Attitudes and Behavior

Environmental psychology researchers and a statistical collaborator are studying why environmental concern does not always translate into sustainable behavior.

Project
Environmental concern and sustainable behavior
Collaborators
Environmental psychology researchers and statistical collaborator
Engagement period
2026 academic year
Version
1

Vision — Q1

Advance understanding of why environmental concern does not always translate into sustainable behavior.

Impact — Q1

Determine whether the attitude-behavior gap depends on context, measurement, and access to sustainable choices, helping researchers design future studies and more effective interventions.

Partnership — Q1

All collaborators will appear as co-authors on a paper targeting environmental psychology journals. The team agrees that null or mixed findings remain valuable and will still be submitted for publication.

Analysis Pathway — Q2

Use multilevel regression to determine how the relationship between environmental concern and behavior varies by context, measurement approach, and access to sustainable choices. Compare results across measures and settings to help researchers identify priorities for future studies and promising targets for behavioral interventions.

Narrative — Q3

Environmental concern was more strongly associated with self-reported than observed behavior, particularly when sustainable alternatives were difficult to access. Researchers can prioritize access and measurement in their next study and design interventions that reduce practical barriers rather than focusing only on attitudes.

What this illustrates: Academic impact may involve contributions to a body of knowledge, decisions about what to study next, and the design of future interventions rather than an immediate organizational outcome.
